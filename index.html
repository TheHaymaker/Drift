Haiku
Japan
SUMMARY
Three-line Japanese form capturing a moment in nature through a seasonal image and a cutting juxtaposition.
HISTORY
Evolved from the opening verse of collaborative linked-verse poetry in 17th-century Japan. Matsuo Bashō elevated it to a standalone art form.
ETHOS
Presence, brevity, seasonal awareness. The haiku seeks to dissolve the boundary between observer and observed.
CONSTRAINTS
Traditional: requires kigo (seasonal word) and kireji (cutting word).
NOTABLE POETS
Matsuo Bashō, Yosa Buson, Kobayashi Issa, Masaoka Shiki
